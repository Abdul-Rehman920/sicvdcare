The record only exists in one place — the file the patient is carrying.

Most cardiovascular units still run entirely on paper. It works, until it doesn't — and the missing history is the patient standing in front of you.

One physical copy
Every ECG and lab report lives inside a single paper file. If it's misplaced, torn, or left at home, that history is gone for good — there's no second copy to fall back on.
Carried by the patient
The patient holds their own record between visits. A returning patient without their file means starting from zero — repeating tests, guessing at a baseline, losing time a cardiac case doesn't have.
No access outside the ward
A doctor reviewing a case from home, or a second opinion from another department, has to wait for the physical file to be walked over before any decision can move forward.

Questions hospitals usually ask

Do we need to buy a new ECG machine?+

No. SICVD Care works with the printout your existing machine already produces — nothing new to install on the ward.

Is patient data actually secure?+

Yes. Every record is encrypted in transit and at rest, access is limited by staff role, and every file open is logged.

How long does it take to set up?+

Most wards are up and running within a few days — the process is mainly registering staff accounts and existing patients.

What happens if the internet goes down?+

The technician can still capture the ECG as usual and upload it once the connection is back — nothing is lost in the meantime.

Can more than one doctor view a file at the same time?+

Yes. Any authorised doctor can open a patient's record independently, from their own device, at the same time as a colleague.
